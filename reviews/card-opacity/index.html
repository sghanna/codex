Recommendation

Selected: 30%.

The darker Crimson gives us more room for shading. Your selected 30% overlay keeps red ink at 4.95:1 contrast. At 40%, the cards are more clearly shaded and red contrast is 4.15:1. At 50%, red is down to 3.35:1; 60–70% falls below 3:1. These are design tradeoffs, not WCAG-approved opacity levels.

What WCAG says

  • WCAG sets contrast ratios, not opacity levels: 4.5:1 for ordinary text and 3:1 for large text at AA. SC 1.4.3
  • Meaningful graphics, such as suit symbols, generally need 3:1 against adjacent colors. SC 1.4.11
  • Inactive controls have contrast exceptions. That does not make faint cards a good choice here: the hand still contains information needed to plan future plays. I would use the readable-content targets for this decision.
  • For stronger readability, AAA raises the text targets to 7:1 for ordinary text and 4.5:1 for large text. With Crimson, 20% and 30% retain at least 4.5:1 for red ink over the darker paper. The 40% and 50% options exceed 3:1 but fall below 4.5:1. SC 1.4.6
  • Keep the written instruction and playable count. The shading should support the rule explanation, so distinguishing the cards does not depend on subtle color differences alone. SC 1.4.1

Compare at Mom’s usual screen brightness and reading distance. In particular, check whether she can still distinguish the shaded Q, J, 10, hearts, and diamonds comfortably.
